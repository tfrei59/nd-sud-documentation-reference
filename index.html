Overview

This page covers documentation rules for licensed SUD treatment programs in North Dakota. It quotes the ND Administrative Code (NDAC) where possible and keeps state requirements separate from ND Medicaid policy, federal rules, and ASAM or accreditation best practice.

How the ND rules are organized

NDAC Article 75-09.1 “Substance Abuse Treatment Programs” is the licensing standard enforced by the ND Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), Behavioral Health Division. Chapter 75-09.1-01 (General Standards) holds nearly all documentation requirements: assessment, the individual treatment plan, continued stay, discharge/transfer criteria, referrals, and client records. Chapters -02 through -08 set the definition, provider, program, and admission criteria for each level of care. Chapter -09 covers DUI Seminars (Level 0.5), and chapter -10 covers Opioid Treatment Programs.

Important caveats before you rely on this page
  • ND ruleChapter 75-09.1-01 defines “ASAM patient placement criteria” as “the third edition, revised” (§75-09.1-01-01(1)). Its record-content rule, however, calls for a six-dimension assessment under “the current version” of the ASAM criteria (§75-09.1-01-22(5)(c)). The rules still use 3rd-edition level names and dimensions, and they refer to a “five-axis” DSM assessment (§§75-09.1-01-14(5), -22(5)(c)) even though DSM-5 dropped the multiaxial system.
  • Draft onlyIn 2024, HHS circulated draft revisions to Article 75-09.1 to align with ASAM 4th edition, DSM-5, and current state and federal law (comments were due June 12, 2024). As of October 10, 2026, the official code on ndlegis.gov still shows the 2004/2018 text for chapters 01–08, so the draft is not in effect. Watch for adoption: deadlines and review frequencies may change.
  • Best practiceASAM is not a regulator. Where this page cites ASAM 4th-edition practice, CARF, or Joint Commission norms, those are not ND legal requirements unless your payer, contract, or accreditor makes them binding.
  • Your program's written policies can be stricter than the rules, and ND requires programs to adopt several of these policies (e.g., timeframes for record entries). Follow your program policy when it is stricter.
LAC scope that touches documentation (NDCC 43-45 & NDAC Title 4.5)
“Counseling or assessment regarding an individual's use or abuse of alcohol or a controlled substance must be provided by a licensed addiction counselor as required by North Dakota Century Code chapter 43-45. The provision of case management and educational services do not need to be performed by a licensed addiction counselor. A licensed addiction counselor must be present in all team meetings at which level of care and treatment planning decisions are made regarding a client receiving or referred for substance abuse treatment services.”NDAC §75-09.1-01-15(6); same language for Level I at §75-09.1-07-03(5)
  • ND lawNDCC §43-45-01(1) defines “addiction counseling” as counseling or assessment regarding a substance-related or addictive disorder identified by the DSM. Under §43-45-05.2, only licensees may practice it or represent themselves as addiction counselors. NDCC ch. 43-45
  • ND ruleLACs must follow the most recently published NAADAC Code of Ethics, which includes duties on records and confidentiality. NDAC §4.5-02.1-05-01 (amended Oct 1, 2025)
  • ND silentNeither NDCC 43-45 nor NDAC Title 4.5 sets treatment plan or discharge summary deadlines. Those come from the program licensing rules (75-09.1) and payer policy.

1. Discharge summaries

What a discharge or transfer summary must contain, when it is due, and how it connects to the discharge and transfer criteria.

Required contents (ND rule)
“Discharge summary describing in measurable and behavioral terms the client's progress and attainment of treatment plan goals and criteria for discharge. When the client is transferred, the discharge summary must include a discharge plan which identifies the treatment goals not yet achieved as well as any problems that have been deferred for treatment by a subsequent provider.”NDAC §75-09.1-01-22(5)(t)
“Transfer summary describing in measurable and behavioral terms a client's move from one level of care to another.”NDAC §75-09.1-01-22(5)(s)
“If admission, ongoing care, or discharge criteria as described by the department have not been met, the provider must document the grounds for placement, ongoing care, or discharge decisions.”NDAC §75-09.1-01-22(5)(u)
“All referrals and recommendations must be made part of the treatment or discharge plan.” A program must also have a written policy that “verifies appropriate referral during and after treatment.”NDAC §75-09.1-01-21(1)–(2)
Timing: when is the discharge summary due?
ND silentThe ND rules set no number of days for completing a discharge or transfer summary. Instead, each program “shall implement a written policy that specifies time frames for entries into the records of a client, such as clinical information, critical incidents or interactions, progress notes, and discharge summaries” (NDAC §75-09.1-01-22(6)). Your program policy sets the deadline. Look it up and follow it.
  • ND MedicaidRecords must “fully justify and disclose the extent of services provided and billed” and be kept “for at least 7 years after the last date the claim was paid or denied.” ND Medicaid SUD Services policy (updated April 2026), Documentation Requirements.
  • ND MedicaidThe ND Medicaid SUD Service Authorization Request (SFN 79) states that, effective November 1, 2008, discharge documentation is required by ND Medicaid. It gives no completion deadline.
  • Best practiceAccreditors and many payers expect discharge summaries to be completed promptly after discharge. Program policies often set a limit such as 7–30 days. That range is a common convention, not an ND requirement, and this research did not verify a specific CARF or Joint Commission number.
  • Draft onlyThe 2024 HHS draft revisions propose more detailed transfer and discharge documentation, including for unplanned discharges. They are not adopted.
Discharge & transfer criteria to document (ND rule)

“Before a program may transfer or discharge a client, the client must have” met one of these criteria (NDAC §75-09.1-01-20):

  1. Goals achieved: “Achieved the goals articulated in the client's individualized treatment plan and resolved the problems that justified admission to the present level of care with progress evaluated and a determination has been made that the client is ready for a less intensive level of care or independent living.”
  2. No further progress likely: “Been unable to resolve the problems that justified admission to the present level of care despite amendments to the treatment plan and no further progress is likely indicating the need for another level of care or type of service.”
  3. Lack of capacity: “Demonstrated a lack of capacity to resolve problems indicating the need for another level of care or type of service.”
  4. Intensification or new problems: “Experienced an intensification of problems or has developed new problems and can be treated effectively only at a more intensive level of care.”

If none of these fits (for example, an against-advice or administrative discharge), document the grounds for the decision under §75-09.1-01-22(5)(u). ND Medicaid repeats the same four criteria in its SUD policy. Section 75-09.1-01-20 does not apply to opioid treatment programs (NDAC §75-09.1-10-02(1)(a)).

2. Master (individual) treatment plan

ND rules call it the “individual treatment plan” or “individualized plan.” Many programs call it the master treatment plan.

Deadlines after admission (ND rule)
“A program shall develop and document an individual treatment plan that is as comprehensive as possible given the time in treatment and the client's condition. The individual treatment plan shall be developed according to the following schedule: a. By the end of the first day for a client in a social detoxification program; b. By the end of the third session for a client receiving outpatient services or intensive outpatient treatment; and c. By the end of the fifth working day for a client receiving day treatment, inpatient, or low-intensity and high-intensity residential treatment.”NDAC §75-09.1-01-15(3); adopted by reference in the ND Medicaid SUD policy (Plan of Care)

“Working day” and “session” are not defined in ch. 75-09.1-01. Count conservatively and define both terms in program policy.

Who participates and who signs
  • ND ruleThe client participates. The program develops the plan “with each client's participation” (§-15(2)), and goals reflect “the client's informed choice” (§-15(1)). Problems identified in the assessment are prioritized and addressed in the plan “with the involvement of the client” (§75-09.1-01-14(6)).
  • ND ruleClient signature, or documentation of involvement. The record must include “the individual plan of the client, including the overall plan and the plans for specific services and signature of the client or other documentation of the client's involvement in the plan” (§-22(5)(o)). It must also show “evidence of the direct involvement of the client in the decisionmaking process” (§-22(5)(l)).
  • ND ruleAn LAC must be present “in all team meetings at which level of care and treatment planning decisions are made” (§-15(6)). The record must designate “the case manager, licensed addiction counselor, and other staff for the client” (§-22(5)(k)).
  • ND ruleAdolescents: “every reasonable attempt to engage an adolescent's family members or guardian in the adolescent's assessment, treatment, and continuing care” (§75-09.1-01-17(4)).
  • ND ruleLevel III.1: the program “shall collaborate with care providers to develop an individual treatment plan for each client with time-specific goals and objectives” (§75-09.1-02-02(6)).
  • ND Medicaid“The ND Medicaid-enrolled rendering or attending provider overseeing the services must approve the plan of care.” The plan must also include the number of programming hours for the level of care (ND Medicaid SUD policy).
  • ND silentCh. 75-09.1-01 does not expressly require a counselor or clinician signature on the plan. In contrast, progress notes must be “signed and dated” (§-22(5)(g)). Medicaid “approval” is best shown by a dated provider signature. Most programs, CARF, and The Joint Commission expect the plan to be signed and dated by the responsible clinician.
Required contents (ND rule)
  • Comprehensive, coordinated, and individualized, “based on referral and assessment information about the client's strengths, abilities, needs, functional deficits, and preferences” (§-15(2)).
  • Addresses problems from the assessment, prioritized by severity (§-14(6)). The assessment covers the ASAM six dimensions and the DSM diagnosis (§-14(5); §-22(5)(c)).
  • Includes the overall plan and the plans for specific services (§-22(5)(o)).
  • Objectives are measurable, so that progress notes can describe progress “in measurable and behavioral terms” (§-14(7); §-22(5)(g)).
  • Services essential to the client's goals are provided, or the record documents attempts to provide them through staff, affiliation, consultation, or referral (§-15(7)).
  • Referrals and recommendations are written into the plan (§-21(1)).

3. Treatment plan reviews & updates

Minimum frequencies for analyzing progress and updating the plan, plus how often progress notes must be written.

The review standard (ND rule)
“A program must regularly analyze with the active involvement of the client the client's progress toward the accomplishment of goals and modify goals and services as a result of any occurrence that is likely to impact the client's treatment progress. A program must perform such an analysis no less often than: a. Once every two months or every eight sessions, whichever comes first, for a client receiving outpatient services; b. Once a month for a client receiving low-intensity residential treatment; c. Once every two weeks for a client receiving intensive outpatient treatment; d. Once each week for a client receiving high-intensity residential, inpatient, or partial hospitalization or day treatment; and e. Once during the first six months and annually thereafter for a client receiving chronic care and maintenance services.”NDAC §75-09.1-01-15(5)

What every review must show: (1) the client's active involvement; (2) analysis of progress toward each goal; (3) changes to goals and services after any significant occurrence (relapse, new diagnosis, legal or housing change, and so on), not only at the scheduled interval; and (4) a continued-stay justification under §75-09.1-01-19, or a transfer or discharge decision under §-20.

Continued-stay documentation at each review (ND rule)

“For a client to remain in the current level of care placement, a program must document that the client” meets one of these (§75-09.1-01-19):

  1. “Is making progress but has not yet achieved the goals articulated in the individualized treatment plan and continued treatment at the present level of care is necessary to permit the client to continue to work toward treatment goals.”
  2. “Is not yet making progress but has the capacity to resolve problems and is actively working toward the goals articulated in the individual treatment plan.”
  3. “New problems have been identified that are appropriately treated at the present level of care that is the least intensive in which these problems can be addressed effectively.”

5. Level-of-care change walkthrough

Work through the six dimensions to build a documentation list for a step-up, a step-down, continued stay, or discharge. Nothing you enter leaves this page.

What ND requires when the level changes
  • ND ruleA transfer summary “describing in measurable and behavioral terms a client's move from one level of care to another” (§-22(5)(s)). When transferring out, the discharge summary must list goals not yet achieved and problems deferred (§-22(5)(t)).
  • ND ruleOne §-20 criterion must be met before the transfer. Criteria 1 and 2 cover step-down or a change of service type; criteria 3 and 4 cover a change to another level or type of care, including a more intensive level.
  • ND ruleThe client must meet admission criteria at the receiving level: a DSM SUD diagnosis plus “specifications in each of the ASAM dimensions required for the recommended level of care” (§-18), and the receiving chapter's admission section (e.g., §75-09.1-03-04 for III.5).
  • ND ruleAn LAC must be present in the team meeting deciding level of care (§-15(6)). Document the client's involvement (§-22(5)(l)) and team conference notes (§-22(5)(m)).
  • ND ruleA new or updated plan is due by the receiving level's deadline (§-15(3)). ND does not say whether a transfer within the same program restarts the clock; set this in program policy.
  • ND ruleIf the placement departs from the department's criteria (for example, because the needed level is unavailable), document the grounds (§-22(5)(u)).
  • FederalAn external referral needs Part 2-compliant consent and the redisclosure notice (§-21(3); 42 CFR 2.31–2.32).

Example timeline: adult Level III.5 residential admission

Before admission: DSM SUD diagnosis and a six-dimension ASAM assessment showing the client meets III.5 admission criteria (§-18; §75-09.1-03-04; §-14).
Day 1 onward: Progress notes at least weekly (§-22(6)(e)). Program policy may require more often.
By end of 5th working day: Individual treatment plan developed with the client, signed by the client or with involvement documented. LAC present at the planning meeting (§-15(3)(c), (6); §-22(5)(o)).
Every week: Plan review with the client's active involvement and continued-stay criteria documented (§-15(5)(d); §-19).
Any significant event: Modify goals and services right away (§-15(5)).
Transfer or discharge: §-20 criterion met. Transfer or discharge summary in measurable, behavioral terms, with goals not yet achieved and deferred problems if transferred. Referrals in the discharge plan (§-20; §-22(5)(s)–(t); §-21(1)).
After discharge: Discharge summary completed within the program-policy timeframe (§-22(6)). Records kept at least 7 years after the last claim was paid or denied (ND Medicaid).

7. Confidentiality: 42 CFR Part 2 and ND release rules

Key points for discharge and transfer records
  • FederalThe 2024 Part 2 final rule (89 FR 12472, Feb 16, 2024) had a compliance date of February 16, 2026, so it now applies. Under it, a single consent may cover all future uses and disclosures for treatment, payment, and health care operations (TPO). Each disclosure made with consent must carry the §2.32 notice. 42 CFR Part 2 (eCFR)
  • FederalConsent elements are in §2.31. SUD counseling notes need separate consent. Part 2 records cannot be used in proceedings against the patient without consent or a court order (§2.12(d), Subpart E).
  • ND ruleReleases must be limited to the information necessary for the referral's purpose, and released records must be stamped or marked to show that further disclosure is prohibited unless the client or legal representative authorizes it (§75-09.1-01-21(3)). The record must include “signed and dated release forms” (§-22(5)(r)).
  • ND ruleClients have the right to “receive notice of federal confidentiality requirements” (§75-09.1-01-23(1)(e)). Programs need a written policy on how a client can access their own record (§-22(2)).

8. Sources, gaps & disclaimer

Where ND rules are silent or unclear
  • ND silentDischarge and transfer summary deadline: no number of days. The program's written policy must set it (§-22(6)).
  • ND silentClinician signature on the treatment plan is not expressly required. Client signature, or documentation of involvement, is required (§-22(5)(o)), and Medicaid requires provider approval.
  • ND silentPlan review interval for social detoxification and progress-note frequency for social detox, PHP adolescent variants, and OTPs.
  • ND silentNo ND licensing chapter for 3.3, ambulatory withdrawal management (1-WM/2-WM), 4th-edition 1.0/1.5/1.7/2.7, or Level 4.
  • ND silent“Working day” and “session” are undefined. It is also unclear whether an internal level change restarts the plan deadline.
  • Edition conflict: the rules define ASAM as the “third edition, revised” (§-01(1)) but require a six-dimension assessment under the “current version” (§-22(5)(c)). They also still cite a five-axis DSM assessment. Ask HHS Behavioral Health Licensing how surveyors apply these until the revisions are adopted.
  • Accreditation: CARF and Joint Commission timeframes were not verified from primary standards, because those manuals are proprietary. Check your accreditor's current manual.
